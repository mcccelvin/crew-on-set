# CAM FX3 viewfinder

`CameraHUDController` displays the recording camera through a 16:9 RenderTexture inside a charcoal surround. Its resource prefab is `Assets/Resources/CameraHUD.prefab`, authored on the next editor refresh by `Assets/Editor/CameraHUDBuilder.cs`. Use **Crew-On-Set > UI > Rebuild CAM FX3 Viewfinder** to rebuild it. The builder preserves an existing prefab by default, so Inspector layout changes survive reloads. The runtime layout is a fallback until the prefab is authored.

`Assets/Resources/CameraHUDArt.asset` references the user's exported `Assets/UI/CAM_FX3` focus and exposure artwork. Changing readouts are TMP labels rather than baked text from the PSD. The original PSD and PNG exports are unchanged.

The recording timer is centred in the top instrument header, in a dark copper-outlined chip with enlarged bold, fixed-width digits. Elapsed recording time displays `HH:MM:SS.mmm`; milliseconds use a slightly smaller copper suffix. SD/identity stay on the left and format/record status move to the right, with separate non-overlapping bounds. The clock remains outside the live image and subject-focus square. It uses the existing elapsed-time state for both single-player and multiplayer; recording duration, capture frame rate and saved footage are unchanged. Runtime styling updates existing viewfinder prefabs without a rebuild.

The held-camera control list retains its paper card, consistent row spacing and outlined gold key badges. The viewfinder instead uses a charcoal/slate instrument palette, off-white readouts and restrained copper accents. A slim identity/SD/format/status/time header and shortcut strip sit outside the 16:9 live image; focus mode/distance share a compact bottom-left ribbon, with level-specific exposure telemetry and matching settings panel. Viewfinder tutorial hints receive the slate style only while the view is open, restoring normal paper styling afterward. The gold/red subject-focus square, tracking geometry and detection callbacks are unchanged by this redesign. Recording warnings remain red, settings/SD capacity reflect real state, and UI is still excluded from recorded footage.

`FilmCameraItem.HUD.cs` supplies real camera settings and recording state. `FilmCameraItem.Settings.cs` supplies the F2 menu. Manual focus unlocks in L2, camera WB in L3, exposure in L4. The UI shows the recorder's actual dimensions and frame rate; it does not invent battery drain or claim the artwork's 60p specification.

The F2 panel is a compact runtime instrument card over the existing authored settings panel: charcoal/slate surfaces, copper outline/top rule, a small adjustment icon, LIVE status, separate outlined value/key badges and a highlighted current row. Composition, colour and exposure sections appear only when unlocked. Its height follows the available 1/3/6 controls, and uniform fit keeps it inside the viewport above telemetry. Selected-control explanations, arrow-selection/adjustment badges and an unscaled opening fade replace the long plain text block and empty fixed-height area. `CameraHUDController.State` receives real grid/tint/selection/open state from the film-camera HUD; adjustment logic, unlocks, saved keys, focus square, input ownership and recorded effects are unchanged. The existing serialized menu label and string-state entry point remain for legacy callers; no prefab rebuild is required.

Camera effects select the actual active renderer. The built-in path uses a private Post Processing Stack v2 profile on the film camera's existing layer (or initializes a new layer from `Resources/FilmCameraPostProcessResources`), mapping white balance/tint to ColorGrading, ISO/aperture/shutter to HDR post-exposure, aperture/focus to depth of field and shutter to supported motion blur. Its high-priority volume is activated only for that film camera's render and disabled after it, on viewfinder close and after manual capture; original layer settings are restored on cleanup. URP retains its Universal Volume path when a real URP asset is active. Neither path changes project pipeline settings, material assets or saved camera-control keys; the recorder's existing prepare/render/finish bracket applies the same look to captured frames.

Contract 3's white-balance practice starts at camera WB 5600K/Tint 0 (the renderer's no-correction baseline), compares camera 3200K and 6500K, then lets the player choose a final balance and close F2. It no longer forces a return to 3200K or calls any number universally best. Matte neutral-grey reference strips on the reflective practice subject provide a colour reference; the shared material is destroyed with the practice setup. Source-light Kelvin and camera correction are taught separately. The warm 3200K lamp exercise and existing lighting scores remain unchanged; camera WB has no exact-value contract gate. The Kelvin-to-post-processing mapping remains a teaching approximation, not an automatic measured white balance. Almanac quick/full notes and the acceptance reminder teach reference-based judgement and consistent balance across the three car takes. No tests were run for this tutorial change, as requested.

Owned live previews and `TruePixelRecorder` now share `FilmCameraItem.RenderCameraFrame`: prepare effects, render via the actual pipeline, finish and restore the previous target/active texture. `CameraHUDController` renders at execution order 300 after the equipment's order-100 pose/lens update, instead of relying on automatically rendered preview frames whose volume setup can differ from capture. While the view is open it disables only that owned camera's automatic rendering; close restores its original enabled state, target, viewport and aspect. Unowned legacy/multiplayer previews retain their automatic path. Camera framing and subject-focus UI remain unchanged.

Single-player SD status shows used seconds out of the card's shared 60-second capacity. Normal takes leave the card inserted; C ejects it for computer ingestion and reuse. Recording stops at the remaining frame capacity. The initial tutorial still automatically ejects its first take for the computer lesson. Computer EDIT renames raw media and its evidence sidecar; DELETE requires confirmation and frees the same card's space. Legacy single-take cards migrate on inspection; exported final videos are not deleted by these controls.

Recording cards use the shared cream paper and brown ink, with a filename row above separate EDIT/DELETE buttons. Names remain single-line with ellipsis and reserved padding; the old blue card chrome is hidden at runtime without editing its source artwork. `CrewPaperStyle.ActionButton` gives EJECT SD a gold fill and brown outline, and styles recording actions/dialog buttons consistently while retaining their existing listeners and disabled states.

Each registered recording card shows a gold SD CARD 1/2/... badge, resolved from its actual owning `SDCardItem` by `ComputerStation.GetSourceCardNumber`, not guessed from filenames. New cards receive their number during successful shop delivery, in purchase order, and retain it on the physical card across rename/delete, grid reuse and ejection/reinsertion, regardless of which card was inserted first. Numbering is scoped to the current career/room session; pre-existing unnumbered cards receive a fallback number when first identified, because historical purchase order was not stored. The badge has its own footer space away from filename/actions; tape/metadata/save formats are unchanged.

EJECT SD opens a paper-styled dropdown below the button, sorted by purchase number, with each card's name, clip count and used seconds. Selecting a row calls `ComputerStation.TryEjectCard` for only that card, refreshes the grid/capacity and keeps all other cards inserted. Outside-click, view changes and window close dismiss the dropdown; keyboard-close protection and the first editing lesson's ejection restriction remain intact. Lists longer than six cards scroll. Card inspection and world pickup prompts use the same display name without changing internal equipment identifiers.

SD cards normalize their world physics before the equipment pickup snapshot and after every drop/device release: dynamic Rigidbody, gravity/collision enabled, cleared motion and continuous collision detection. Their solid pickup box follows the imported mesh's actual local bounds with a minimum 2.5 cm world thickness, rather than relying on an offset/thin authored collider. Shop delivery still temporarily freezes a positioned card; inventory pickup disables its colliders normally. Camera ejection no longer freezes a loose card before it returns to inventory. Recordings, scores and physical-card/source identity stay on the same object.

The computer's E close shortcut goes through `ComputerStation.CloseFromShortcut` and is blocked while a rename/delete file dialog is open, including the frame just after dismissal. Typing E therefore stays in the filename; Save Name, Cancel and explicit close buttons keep their existing behavior.

`MultiplayerAuthoredUI` uses the same layout with Photon recording time and actual SD state. Multiplayer currently records 320x180 at 6 fps and does not implement the single-player exposure controls, so those readouts remain hidden.

Existing scene `Cam Pov` artwork is suppressed while the new view is active. Thirds grid and subject tracking move into the inset view. TruePixelRecorder already restores a previous camera target after capture, so UI/borders never enter the tape. Closing the view restores the original camera target, viewport and aspect ratio.
